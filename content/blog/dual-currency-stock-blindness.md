# Dual currency stock blindness: when CDF and USD hide what you sold

At close, the drawer holds francs and dollars. The notebook holds numbers without currency tags. Stock left all day. Tomorrow's order will be a guess. Dual currency is normal in Kinshasa. Untagged dual currency is how margins and quantities lie to you every night.

## Definition

**Dual currency stock blindness** is selling in CDF and USD without recording which currency paid which line, so cash stories and quantity stories diverge.

## What doing nothing already costs

| Leak | What you already pay |
|------|----------------------|
| Untagged sale | False margin and confused cash |
| Mixed drawer | Long, stressful close |
| Guessed restock | Stockouts next to overstock |
| Improvised rates | Staff each invent conversion |

Escape, not shame: the people running notebooks and chats were holding the business together. The goal is a calmer system, not a courtroom for past improvisation.

## Framework

1. **Tag** — every sale chooses CDF, USD, or explicit split lines
2. **Decrement** — stock moves by quantity, not by money feeling
3. **Rate card** — print today's reference rate for staff
4. **Close** — count currencies separately before wallets
5. **Restock** — order from quantities sold

## Believable operational scenario

A Gombe accessory counter sells an item as "vingt dollars." The buyer pays a mix of CDF and a mobile transfer. The book says 20$. Fees nibble the wallet. Stock is gone. The owner thinks the week was strong. Cash disagrees. Nobody stole. Tagging failed.

## Common mistakes

- One notebook column labelled money
- Converting ad hoc at each sale
- Ignoring wallet fees in the head
- Restocking from memory after a busy Saturday

## Why quantity must lead money

Cash can be wrong for many reasons. Quantity sold is the cleanest signal for restock. When currency tags are missing, owners "fix" stock with cash feelings and order the wrong SKUs.

## Tools that help

Offline-first till with currency fields, stock counts, printable day summary, Lingala/French/English labels for staff who switch languages at the counter.

## How AI changes this (honestly)

AI can remind, summarise, translate, and flag anomalies. It cannot invent proof you never captured. Design the object and evidence rules first; then let AI reduce chasing. Professionals who can design those rules stay valuable. Students should practise object design, not only chatbot demos.

## FAQ

**Should we refuse USD?**

Most Kinshasa counters cannot. Tagging beats refusal theater.

**What about rate changes midday?**

Update the posted card when you decide; do not let each cashier invent a round number.

**Does this matter for tiny kiosks?**

Yes — tiny kiosks feel cash gaps harder.


## Why this leak compounds weekly

Every week you postpone the object, the improvisations multiply. New staff learn the workaround. Old staff invent exceptions. Leadership sees a calm surface until a dispute, an audit, or a stockout forces reconstruction. The cost is not dramatic every day. It is steady — evenings, trust chips, and cash in the wrong place.

Date one incident. Then date the next. The calendar is the argument for a pilot.

Capture feels faster than the old habit. Review happens during the week, not only in a crisis. Someone on leave does not trap the only copy of the truth. Leadership can answer the top question without calling three people. Disputes still happen — proof arrives in minutes, not days.

## Teaching adoption without a revolt

Stand beside the person who carries the process in their head. Ask what would make capture easier than their workaround. Build the pilot around that answer. Celebrate matched days between old and new records. Do not ban the old channel on day one; make it unnecessary by day fourteen for the pilot scope.

 
## Metrics that matter (and vanity to ignore)

Matter: time to answer the key question, number of disputes, age of open exceptions, length of close or report build, stockouts tied to visibility. Ignore: raw login counts, messages sent, features enabled. Vanity metrics hide leaks.
