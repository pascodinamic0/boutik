# Credit book leaks: the quiet debt that empties small shops

Credit keeps customers when cash is short. Unrecorded credit keeps only stories. When debt lives in a notebook corner or a chat with a regular, collection depends on memory and mood. Stock is already gone. Doing nothing feels polite. It is how shops hollow out quietly.

## Definition

A **credit book leak** is stock leaving without a durable named debt: who, what, how much, and due when.

## What doing nothing already costs

| Leak | What you already pay |
|------|----------------------|
| Oral IOU | Denial later |
| Chat debt | History leaves with the phone |
| No due date | Eternal soft debt |
| No item list | Arguments over what was taken |

Escape, not shame: the people running notebooks and chats were holding the business together. The goal is a calmer system, not a courtroom for past improvisation.

## Framework

1. Name the customer on every credit sale
2. List items and totals
3. Set a due date
4. Review the credit list weekly
5. Pause new credit when overdue
6. Remind privately — dignity beats public shame

## Believable operational scenario

A regular takes goods "until Friday." Friday becomes next month. The merchant cannot refuse because the debt is fuzzy. A named line with a due date would have made the pause fair for both sides.

## Common mistakes

- Treating credit as friendship only
- No weekly review
- Adding new credit on top of overdue
- Keeping the only list on a personal phone

## Tools that help

Credit book beside sales and stock; weekly overdue filter; private reminder workflow.

## Fourteen-day path

Days 1-2: date the loss. Days 3-4: define the object. Days 5-7: capture new events on the object only. Days 8-10: compare disputes and time-to-answer with last week. Days 11-14: retire the shadow process for the pilot scope. If calm does not improve, simplify the tool before blaming staff.

## How AI changes this (honestly)

AI can remind, summarise, translate, and flag anomalies. It cannot invent proof you never captured. Design the object and evidence rules first; then let AI reduce chasing. Professionals who can design those rules stay valuable. Students should practise object design, not only chatbot demos.

## FAQ

**Is credit bad?**

No. Untracked credit is.

**What if customers get offended?**

Clarity is respect. Fuzzy debt creates worse fights later.


## Why this leak compounds weekly

Every week you postpone the object, the improvisations multiply. New staff learn the workaround. Old staff invent exceptions. Leadership sees a calm surface until a dispute, an audit, or a stockout forces reconstruction. The cost is not dramatic every day. It is steady — evenings, trust chips, and cash in the wrong place.

Date one incident. Then date the next. The calendar is the argument for a pilot.

## What good looks like after two weeks

Capture feels faster than the old habit. Review happens during the week, not only in a crisis. Someone on leave does not trap the only copy of the truth. Leadership can answer the top question without calling three people. Disputes still happen — proof arrives in minutes, not days.

## Teaching adoption without a revolt

Stand beside the person who carries the process in their head. Ask what would make capture easier than their workaround. Build the pilot around that answer. Celebrate matched days between old and new records. Do not ban the old channel on day one; make it unnecessary by day fourteen for the pilot scope.

 
## Metrics that matter (and vanity to ignore)

Matter: time to answer the key question, number of disputes, age of open exceptions, length of close or report build, stockouts tied to visibility. Ignore: raw login counts, messages sent, features enabled. Vanity metrics hide leaks.
